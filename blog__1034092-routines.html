Routines

  • posted: Sep. 07, 2023

When I’d start my nursing shift, I always got cleaning wipes and wiped down the desk, computers, handles, etc. It was how I started every shift. One day a nurse said to me, “I am jealous of how you have such a routine that you stick to”. She really had me thinking. I had never thought much about it. I started to notice that I had a lot of routines. I also noticed that others frequently forgot things, missed things that should have gotten done, or literally lost important items. It really made me think about how I follow routines, but never really thought about it that way. For example, I have a little tray that everything goes in when I come into the house and when I leave it all goes with me. So, when I went to nursing shifts there were certain items that I needed in my pockets. My pens, permanent marker, lip balm, and eye drops were all on that tray and all made it to work with me every shift. My badge was there and my keys. I never misplaced my keys because they always went in the same place. This all seems simple. Yet, I frequently hear others become frantic because they can’t find their keys, they lost their badge, or forgot their wallet. Routines can take a little getting used to in the beginning, but by purposely creating a routine around a “problem” you can eliminate that problem.

Let’s look at a few examples. Some people have trouble remembering to take their medications in the morning. Let’s say you enjoy a cup of coffee in the morning, but you don’t like to eat breakfast. Your provider has told you that your medications need to be taken at about the same time each day with a little food. A routine you can establish is that you don’t sit down to your coffee until you have 3 things on the table. Your morning medication, a small snack, and your coffee must all be there before you drink. In the beginning you may need a note by the coffee pot, but within no time you are used to the routine and get your medication and snack on time.

Here is another example. You are single and you really hate doing the dishes. Nobody else is there so you tend to let it go. The problem is eventually the sink is full and you are really hating it now! You can make it routine that you have the dishes done every night before you go to bed. Put a note on the mirror or light switch and just do it. It won’t take long until you do it without thinking about it and it is no longer a stressful issue.

Here is one last example. You have cats. You love your cats. You hate the litter box. You tend to forget to do it daily and when you do remember the box stinks! Choose a time daily that you will always do it. I do mine after breakfast. I have been doing it this way for a while, so I don’t think about it. It is just built into me that I do the animal chores after breakfast.

Small routines can be created to help with anything you want to change. You can build in journal writing, stop misplacing your keys, or get your medications on track by creating a routine. It does not take long until you no longer have to think about it. It becomes automatic.
